SECTION 01
The agreement problem
What two machines must decide together, and why the network will not help.
Section 01
Three properties every protocol must hold
—Agreement. No two correct processes decide on different values.
—Validity. The decided value was proposed by some process; nothing is invented.
—Termination. Every correct process eventually decides, rather than waiting forever.
—Drop any one of the three and the problem becomes easy — which is the whole story of the next hour.

Section 02
Synchronous and asynchronous models
Synchronous
Message delay has a known upper bound. A missing reply after that bound is a failure, not slowness.
Timeouts are evidence. Consensus is achievable with a simple round-based protocol.
Asynchronous
No bound exists. A slow process and a crashed process look identical from the outside.
Timeouts are guesses. This is the model real networks force on us.

What to take away
01Consensus is a bargain between safety and liveness, and you choose which one to give up.

03Raft works in production because it makes the leader explicit, not because it beats the impossibility result.
